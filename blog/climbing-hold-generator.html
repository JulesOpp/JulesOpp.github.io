Climbing Hold Generator

November 21, 2026

A web app to model climbing holds for 3D printing or CNC machining. This is a web version of my desktop Climbing Hold Generator, which is not yet released.

Drag to rotate · scroll or pinch to zoom · double-click to reset. The grid is the wall, in 10 mm squares. Wall angle is degrees past vertical: 0 vertical, 30 a steep overhang, 90 a roof, negative a slab.

      Printing

      Use at your own risk. A 3D printed climbing hold may break. Be careful when designing geometries, as prints can break, especially along layer lines or at thin sections. Mine have had no problems with strength or delamination (using only Polymaker PolyMAX Tough PLA filament), but yours may break under you. Choose the hold with care and think about how torque will be applied by both the screws and body weight. The Result panel is a crude estimate of filament use and printing time, and a crude estimate of how much support the print needs (support marks may be bad for texture).

      Some texture can be built into the geometry, but additional texturing is required. In BambuStudio, a Fuzzy Skin = Contour modifier adds the necessary surface texture. Polymaker PolyMAX PLA works well, and any tough PLA should be good enough. Texture will improve once the hold has been chalked. The screw holes are sized for a #8 (or M4) flat-head wood screw: a 13 mm counterbore, a 90° countersink and a 5 mm clearance hole. Note that holds may break if screwed too tightly, but they must be securely fixed to the wall.

      CNC milling

      The parts may be machined (subtractive rather than additive manufacturing). Machining needs .STEP files rather than .STL, because CAM software requires the true surfaces rather than meshes. The machinable edge is therefore built twice: as a mesh for the preview, and as an exact solid made in OpenCascade for the STEP file. That solid is the convex hull of six equal spheres, grown by their radius. The result is made only of planes, cylinders, cones and spheres, with the fillets as tori and blends, and there is not a single faceted surface in the file.

      I had one milled from 6061 aluminium. I got quotes from Xometry and PCBWay, and the totals came out essentially the same. These are the settings I ordered with:

      ProcessCNC Machining (no preferred subprocess)
      MaterialAluminum 6061-T6
      FinishBead blast
      Threads, tapped holes, insertsnone
      Tolerance±0.010″ (±0.25 mm)
      Surface roughness125 µin / 3.2 µm Ra
      InspectionStandard
      Production typePrototype

      Bead blasting is important for surface friction, as bare machined aluminium is too slick. Avoid sharp edges, as these can cut you.

      Design for how it will be milled

      The price of a one-off milled part is mostly setup and programming, not cutting time or metal, so a one-off part will be expensive. Before ordering, consider how the part will be machined:

      • Keep flats on the sides, so the part can be held in a vice without needing a fixture.
      • Add a plinth. Extruding the mounting face straight back by about 10 mm makes the flats taller and nearly square, which stops the part pivoting in the jaws. It also gives the part a straight base that is easy to machine.
      • Keep concave corners large. Every rounded surface on this hold is convex except the crease where the plinth meets the body. The fillet there sets the largest ball cutter that can finish the part. A 6 mm fillet lets the shop use a 12 mm ball, which makes the finishing toolpath about 30% shorter at a cost of a few cubic millimetres of metal. Tell the shop that the form is convex, so it can use a large cutter.
      • An incut lip cannot be 3-axis milled. Say in the order notes that it needs 3+2 (indexed) or 5-axis machining, so it is not quoted as 3-axis and then re-quoted.
      • Order more than one. At quantity 1 the fixed costs all land on a single part. Quantity is the only lever that changes the price a lot.
      • Mind the screws. The screw seat slider sets how much metal sits behind each screw head. A deep seat leaves a shallower hole in the climbing surface, but the screw then has to pass through that much aluminium before it reaches the wall, so buy longer screws.

      Before you order, print the same shape in PLA and screw it to the wall to check that you like the hold.
