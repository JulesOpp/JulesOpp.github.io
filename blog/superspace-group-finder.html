Superspace Group Finder

November 10, 2026

This page is used for descending (3+d)D superspace groups with rational modulation vectors and a set phase t₀ to their corresponding 3D space group. This follows the (3+1)D to 3D Superspace Group Finder written by Orlov, Schoeni, and Chapuis and utilizes the enumeration of superspace groups from the ISO(3+d)D project at BYU. This page extends to (3+d)D descent for d = 2 and 3.

How to use. The superspace-group tables belong to the ISO(3+d)D project at BYU and are not distributed with this page. Instead you copy over the information yourself:

  1. Type the group's label (e.g. 62.1.9.2) and press Open BYU page. Your browser opens the group's page on iso.byu.edu.
  2. On that page, select everything (Cmd-A or Ctrl-A), copy it, and paste it into the box below.
  3. Enter rational values for the free parameters of the q-vectors and press Find space groups.

Everything is run locally. Your browser will remember the last page you pasted for each group, so a permalink to a result works again later on the same browser.

To list the subgroups of a (3+1)D group first, use the companion page Superspace Subgroups; its Send to finder link opens a subgroup here.

Labels look like 62.1.9.2 (ISO(3+d)D numbering).

Method

Write a superspace operation as g = (R, ε, M; τE, τI), with R its 3D rotation, ε its d×d action on the internal coordinates, and M + εQ = QR, where the rows of Q are the q-vectors. The section of the superspace crystal at phase t₀ is a 3D crystal, and g gives one of its symmetries, combined with the basic lattice translation n, exactly when

(ε−1)t0+τI−Q(τE+n)∈Zd

for some n ∈ Z³. Since t₀ is only defined up to an origin shift, it lives on the torus Rd/Λ with Λ = Zd + QZ³. In a basis of Λ every survival condition becomes an integer congruence, which the Smith normal form solves exactly as a finite union of connected flats (affine subtori). Closing these flats under intersection gives every distinct set of surviving operations; each class is a flat minus its sub-flats, and classes related by the superspace group itself are merged into one row. The loci are printed as congruences in t (e.g. t3 ≡ 1/8 (mod 1/4)).

For each class the surviving operations, at an exact generic point, give the 3D operations in the conventional supercell N₁a × N₂b × N₃c. The space-group type, Hall number and transformation to the standard setting come from moyo (compiled to WebAssembly), applied to a pseudo-structure with exactly that symmetry. A count check flags any failed identification instead of reporting a guess. The symbol in the axes of the superspace group, as the IT finder prints it, is worked out separately from the operations.

Limitations

  • Rows are merged only by the superspace group itself, not by its normaliser. Sections that are different crystals with the same description (for example different origin choices of the basic group) appear as separate rows, as in the IT finder.
  • Magnetic superspace groups are not handled.
  • The symbol in superspace-group axes uses orthogonal symmetry directions, so it is less reliable for hexagonal and trigonal groups; the standard symbol and number from moyo are not affected.
  • The conventional supercell is limited to two million basic cells.
