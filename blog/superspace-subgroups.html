Superspace Subgroups

November 10, 2026

This page lists the subgroups of a (3+1)D superspace group that keep its full superspace lattice: the subgroups of k-index 1, also called translationengleiche subgroups. It follows the layout of CELLSUB on the Bilbao Crystallographic Server, one row per conjugacy class, and names the type of each subgroup in the style of the ISO(3+d)D project at BYU. It is a companion to the Superspace Group Finder: any row can be sent there to find the 3D space groups of its commensurate sections.

Scope for now: d = 1 (one modulation vector) and k-index 1 only. Subgroups that lose lattice translations (k-index > 1) and groups with d = 2 or 3 are refused rather than answered wrongly.

How to use. The superspace-group tables belong to the ISO(3+d)D project at BYU and are not distributed with this page. As on the finder, open the group's BYU page, select everything (Cmd-A or Ctrl-A), copy it and paste it below, then press List subgroups. Everything is run locally in your browser.

Reading the table

  • Index is the index of the subgroup H in the parent G. With k-index 1 it equals the t-index, the index of the point group of H in that of G.
  • Point group and Basic SG are those of H's basic space group, in the type's setting (number in brackets as in International Tables A).
  • Superspace group (type) is the type of H, written as BYU writes it: the q family of the Bravais class in letters (a, b, g) and one phase letter per symmetry direction (0, s = 1/2, t = 1/3, q = 1/4, h = 1/6). Where several orthorhombic settings are tied, the other names are given after ≡; they are the same type.
  • q is the subgroup's own modulation vector in that setting, a member of the type's q family (for example (0,0,g) in a P-1(a,b,g) type).
  • Conjugates is the number of subgroups in the conjugacy class; Maximal says whether no other k-index-1 subgroup lies between H and G.
  • Transformation gives the coordinates (x′, y′, z′; t′) of the type's setting in terms of the parent's (x, y, z; t). Press the index to see the generators of H in the parent's axes, its symbol in those axes when the crystal system is unchanged, and the Send to finder link.

Method

Write the point group of G as pairs (R, ε) with R its 3D rotation and ε = ±1 its action on the internal coordinate. For an incommensurate q the pair (1, −1) is never in this group, so ε is a function of R and the point group is isomorphic to the point group K of the basic space group. A subgroup of G with the same superspace lattice is then the full preimage of a subgroup P of K, and two such subgroups are conjugate in G exactly when their P are conjugate in K. So the rows are the conjugacy classes of subgroups of K, and q is unchanged.

Each subgroup is identified from its operations: the basic space group by moyo (compiled to WebAssembly), then a search over settings of that space group for the one that brings q into a Bravais-class q form of International Tables C (Table 9.8.3), in the axis and lattice conventions used by ISO(3+d)D. Among the equivalent names that remain, a fixed choice rule picks one. The type names are worked out by this algorithm, not looked up; the rule was checked offline against the ISO(3+d)D listings. If a subgroup cannot be identified, its row says so and a warning explains why.

Limitations

  • d = 1 and k-index 1 only. CELLSUB's general subgroups (supercells and changes of the internal lattice) are not yet handled.
  • Magnetic superspace groups are not handled.
  • The symbol in the parent's axes is only given when the subgroup keeps the parent's crystal system.
